# Age-band design, pedagogy & safety rules

Read before Stage 0's age-fit check, before Stage 3, and before every render QA. The numbers below are **working defaults**. The user's brief or series bible can override them, but safety rules can't be overridden.

## Universal rules (all ages)

**Teaching**
- **Show it when you say it.** The named object, number, letter or action is on screen, in focus and recognisable *at or slightly before* its word's `start`, never after. Keep it there until at least the word's `end`.
- **One idea at a time.** Introduce → name → reinforce (repeat, or show a second example) → recall (quiz pause or recap).
- **Accuracy is part of quality.** Colours are true (an apple is red or green, not purple), animals look like the real animal and make the right sound, counts on screen exactly equal the number said, letters and Devanagari characters have correct shapes and **stroke order**, facts are current and correct. Note anything uncertain in `accuracyNotes` and ask the user.
- **Recap at the end** of the key vocabulary or concepts, in the same order and with the same visuals as when they were introduced.
- **Interaction pauses:** when the narrator asks ("Can you find the red one?"), the frame holds and simplifies, a gentle waiting cue plays (the mascot looks at the camera, a soft pulse on the candidates), then the answer is revealed on the narrator's answer word.

**Graphics first, text last**
- Children 0–9 learn from **pictures + voice**, not reading. The narration says it; the picture **shows** it.
- Every concept is shown as an **object, character, action, expression or simple diagram**, never as a sentence on screen.
  - Nouns become the thing itself.
  - Verbs become a character doing it.
  - Feelings become faces and body language.
  - Comparisons become two things side by side.
  - Sequences become steps that happen one after another.
  - Rules ("wash your hands") become the mascot doing it right, with a ✓ and a smile.
- **No subtitles or narration captions** in the final video.
- **Text budget** (the maximum allowed):

| Age | Allowed on-screen text |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | **None.** The picture and the voice are enough. (Optional: a tiny brand mark.) |
| 3–6 | **Only the letter or number being taught**, treated as a big graphic shape (traced, coloured, turned into an object). At most one short word when it's the actual teaching target (e.g. "CAT" in a phonics video). No sentences. |
| 6–9 | **1–3-word labels** on diagrams (e.g. "root", "stem", "leaf"; "₹10") and numbers in maths. No sentences, captions or text definitions; the definition is shown as a visual. |

- **No-text test:** hide every piece of text. If the scene no longer teaches with the audio on, redesign it with pictures.
- Use symbols children already know instead of words: ✓ / ✗, arrows, a big friendly "?", a clock face, ₹ coins, a thumbs-up.

**Safety (non-negotiable)**
- **Photosensitivity:** no more than 3 flashes per second, no large full-frame luminance or saturated-red flashes, no high-contrast flickering stripes or checkerboards. Transitions use moves, wipes-by-object or soft dissolves, never strobe cuts.
- **Nothing scary or harmful:** no menacing faces, sudden loud visual "jump" moments, injury, weapons, dangerous imitable acts (climbing on furniture, objects in the mouth, playing with fire or knives), or adult themes. Wild animals are friendly but realistic (a tiger is a tiger, not a monster).
- **No overstimulation:** limited simultaneous motion, calm backgrounds behind the teaching object, no constant shake or zoom, no clutter.
- **Inclusive and culturally grounded:** Indian context (names, foods, festivals, homes, clothes, places) where relevant. Diverse skin tones, genders and family types. No stereotypes.
- **Original assets only:** no copyrighted characters, logos or imagery.
- **Platform:** treat the content as made for kids. No calls to external links, no ad-like persuasion, no personal-data prompts.

**Language** (applies only to the little text that is allowed)
- On-screen text matches the script exactly (same script, same spelling).
- For mixed English + Hindi, each word is shown in the script it's spoken in. Optionally add a small secondary label in the other language if the series bible says so.
- Devanagari: correct shaping (libraqm/HarfBuzz), akshara-safe splitting, correct matra positions, Devanagari numerals only if the script uses them.

**Motion & audio**
- Physical, designed motion (anticipation, overshoot, settle). Softer springs and slower timing for younger bands.
- Word timestamps drive the teaching sync. Audio-map (pauses, emphasis, music bed) drives only gentle secondary motion.
- Small SFX-like visual "pops" are fine on emphasis words. Don't pulse on every beat.

## 0–3 years (babies & toddlers, pre-readers)

**What they learn:** first words and objects (fruits, animals and their sounds, body parts, family, vehicles, household things), colours, simple shapes, counting 1–5 (1–10 by 3), big/small, up/down, in/out, emotions (happy/sad), daily routines (eating, bath, sleep), rhymes, Hindi words for the same things.
**How to show it**
- **One hero object, centred and large** (≈50–70% of frame width), on a plain or very calm background. Real, recognisable depiction: simple and rounded, but true to life.
- **Hold each new object ≥3–4 s.** Cuts or scene changes no faster than ~3 s. Slow, smooth moves. Gentle bounce and wobble, no fast spins.
- High contrast, bright but not neon. Limited palette per scene (2–4 colours).
- **No text.** The object, its sound and the narrator's voice do the teaching. The object can also *act out* its word (the ball bounces, the cow says moo, the baby sleeps).
- Faces and eyes attract attention. A friendly guide character, or the object "saying hello" (blink, wave), works well.
- Repetition is a feature: the same object can be named 2–3 times with small variations (a different angle, an action, a sound).
- Cause and effect: tap → pop, push → roll. Clear, predictable outcomes.
- Interaction pauses are short (≈2–3 s), with the answer always revealed.

## 3–6 years (preschool & kindergarten, emerging readers)

**What they learn:** the alphabet (letter names and **phonics** sounds), Hindi varnmala (स्वर/व्यंजन), numbers 1–20 and counting, simple addition with objects, shapes, colours and mixing, opposites, seasons, weather, days of the week, animals and habitats, plants, community helpers, festivals, healthy habits, safety rules, simple stories with a lesson, emotions and manners, simple sequencing and patterns.
**How to show it**
- **Letters and numbers appear only when they are the lesson**, and then as big graphic shapes: **traced with correct stroke order** (English and Devanagari), coloured in, then *morphing into* or *joined by* the object ("B" bounces and becomes a ball).
- **Count-along:** objects appear **one at a time on each number word**, each with a little pop. The numeral is shown only when numbers are the lesson. The final count equals the number said.
- **No read-along text.** Stories, habits, feelings and rules are acted out by characters.
- 2–3 items on screen at most. ≥2 s per new item.
- Simple story scenes with a consistent character. Clear beginning → problem → solution.
- Quiz pauses ≈3–4 s ("Which one is a triangle?"), then the answer is revealed with a celebration.
- Playful but tidy compositions. More colour and character than 0–3, still calm behind the teaching object.

## 6–9 years (early primary, readers)

**What they learn:** reading and vocabulary, grammar basics, place value, addition, subtraction, multiplication tables, fractions, time and money (₹), measurement, the human body and senses, plants (parts, photosynthesis basics), animal classification, life cycles, the water cycle, the solar system, weather, India (states, monuments, festivals, maps), environment and recycling, simple machines, good habits and road safety, simple science experiments, stories and moral values.
**How to show it**
- **Diagrams, labels, processes and comparisons:** life-cycle wheels, labelled body or plant diagrams, simple maps, timelines, charts, step-by-step processes, before/after, vs. layouts.
- Text is limited to **1–3-word labels** pointing at parts of a picture (≈56 px+, high contrast, on a clean area) and numbers in maths. No captions or text definitions.
- Faster pacing is fine, but each new term gets a clear **"show beat"**: the narrator says the term while the picture *demonstrates* it ("evaporation" = water rising as wisps from a warm puddle), with at most a one-word label.
- Visual metaphors and wordplay (the PYAAR? "meaning drives the visual" approach) work best here, as long as they clarify rather than confuse.
- Quiz and recall moments ("Pause and think!") with a 4–5 s pause, then the reveal. End-of-video recap or "3 things you learned".
- A clean, illustrated diagram look is OK (cutaways, arrows, cycles). Diagrams are pictures first, with labels second.

## Age-fit check (used in Stage 0 & Stage 3 self-review)

For each script line or scene, flag:
- vocabulary or concepts clearly above the band (e.g. "photosynthesis" for 0–3)
- concepts too abstract to *show* for the band
- factual errors or outdated facts
- pacing: too many new items per second for the band
- unsafe or imitable behaviour, scary or adult content
- cultural mismatch for Indian kids
- any line that can only be understood by **reading**: redesign it as a picture or action
- text above the band's text budget

Report the flags. **Never rewrite the script without asking.**
